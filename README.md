There is a conversation many couples have only after they get married.

Sometimes weeks after.

Sometimes months after.

And sometimes...

not at all.

It's not about money.

It's not about where they'll live.

It's not even about children.

It's about sex.

And for couples who waited until marriage, the silence can be even more noticeable.

You can spend months planning the wedding.

You discuss the venue.

The guest list.

The clothes.

The food.

The honeymoon.

Where you'll live.

How you'll combine your finances.

You talk about almost everything.

Except the thing that many couples suddenly have to figure out when the wedding is over.

What happens when we're finally alone?





IT SOUNDS LIKE SOMETHING YOU'LL JUST FIGURE OUT.

And maybe you will.

But imagine this.

You've just gotten married.

You're exhausted from the wedding.

You're excited.

You're nervous.

Your spouse is nervous too.

Neither of you really knows what the other person expects.

Neither wants to say the wrong thing.

So instead of talking...

you both assume.

"They'll probably be comfortable with this."

"They'll know what I want."

"We'll just figure it out."

And that's where something interesting happens.

You can love someone deeply...

and still completely misunderstand them.

Because your spouse cannot read your mind.

And you cannot read theirs.





YOUR FIRST NIGHT DOESN'T HAVE TO BE PERFECT.

This is one of the biggest things newlywed couples need to understand.

Your first sexual experience may not look like the version you've imagined.

It might be beautiful.

It might be awkward.

It might be exciting.

It might be completely different from what you expected.

And none of those things automatically means something is wrong.

You're not performing for an audience.

You're not taking an exam.

You're two people beginning to learn each other in a very vulnerable way.

That's why the better question isn't:

"Did we do it right?"

It's:

"Did we feel comfortable enough to communicate?"

That question is much more important.





BECAUSE THE REAL ISSUE ISN'T ALWAYS SEX.

Sometimes it's communication.

One person is nervous but doesn't say anything.

The other person doesn't realize it.

One person expects intimacy to happen frequently.

The other needs more time.

One person thinks something is completely normal.

The other has never even considered it.

Neither person is necessarily wrong.

They simply have different expectations.

And if nobody talks about those differences...

they don't magically disappear.

They become assumptions.

Then misunderstandings.

Then frustration.

And eventually, sometimes, resentment.





THIS IS WHY SOME OF THE MOST IMPORTANT WORDS IN A MARRIAGE CAN SOUND VERY SIMPLE.

"I'm nervous."

"Can we slow down?"

"I'm comfortable with this."

"I'm not comfortable with that."

"Can we talk about it?"

These aren't signs of a bad marriage.

They're signs that two people are actually learning how to communicate.

And that matters because intimacy isn't only physical.

It's trust.

It's vulnerability.

It's feeling safe enough to tell your spouse the truth.

It's knowing that you can say something uncomfortable without being mocked, pressured or dismissed.





AND THEN LIFE HAPPENS.

This is the part nobody can fully prepare you for.

Your first night is only one night.

Your marriage may last decades.

Eventually there will be stressful seasons.

Busy seasons.

Pregnancy.

Children.

Fatigue.

Changes in your body.

Changes in desire.

Health challenges.

Times when one person wants intimacy and the other doesn't.

And moments when you don't even know how to explain what's wrong.

The question isn't whether these things can happen.

The question is:

Will you know how to talk about them when they do?

Because a healthy intimate relationship isn't something you establish once on your wedding night.

It's something you keep building.





THAT'S WHY I WROTE AFTER "I DO."

I didn't want to create another book that simply tells couples that sex is important.

You already know that.

The bigger question is:

How do you actually build a healthy intimate relationship inside a real marriage?

How do you talk about sex without embarrassment?

How do you communicate what you need?

What happens when you and your spouse have different levels of desire?

How do you handle intimacy during stressful seasons?

How do you navigate difficult conversations?

How do emotional connection and physical intimacy affect each other?

And what do you do when something isn't working?

These are the conversations that don't always happen before marriage.

But they matter enormously after it.





AFTER "I DO"

The Complete Guide to Sex, Intimacy & Building a Healthy Sexual Relationship in Marriage

This book was created for couples who don't want to leave one of the most important parts of their marriage to guesswork.

It won't promise you a perfect marriage.

It won't tell you that every couple will experience intimacy the same way.

And it won't pretend that one book can solve every problem.

Instead, it gives you something much more useful:

A framework for understanding, communicating and growing together.

So that when marriage changes...

you know how to keep talking.





YOU DON'T HAVE TO KNOW EVERYTHING BEFORE YOU GET MARRIED.

You just need to be willing to learn.

Learn your spouse.

Learn how to communicate.

Learn how to handle differences.

Learn how to talk about difficult things.

And learn how to build intimacy that goes beyond the wedding night.

Because your wedding day is one day.

Your marriage is everything that comes after.

AFTER "I DO"

The conversations you have after the wedding may shape your marriage more than the wedding itself.
